## Understanding Failure-to-Deliver (FTD): Key Definitions

**What is Failure-to-Deliver (FTD)?**

- FTD occurs when one party in a securities transaction fails to fulfill their contractual obligation by the settlement date.
- Typically relates to:
    - Buyer failing to deliver the cash payment for purchased securities.
    - Seller failing to deliver the actual securities sold.

**How FTD Works in Practice**

- Both buyer and seller must complete obligations by settlement date (usually T+2, two business days after trade).

**Types of FTD Situations**

- **1. Cash-Related FTD (Buyer Side):**
    - Buyer doesn’t have funds to pay (margin call, liquidity crunch).
- **2. Securities-Related FTD (Seller Side):**
    - Seller doesn’t own securities sold (often in short selling).
    - Naked short selling: Selling shares without borrowing first.
- **3. Technical/Operational FTD:**
    - System failures, errors, administrative delays, miscommunications.

***

## Why FTD Data Matters

- **Market Stress Indicator:** High FTD values signal settlement and market stress.
- **Settlement System Health:** FTD data reveals structural and manipulation risks.
- **SEC Oversight:**
    - FTD data published for transparency.
    - Monitors “threshold securities”—stocks with high FTDs.
    - Regulation SHO enforces against abusive naked short selling.
    - FTD data reported if >10,000 shares or >0.5% of outstanding shares fail for 5+ days.

***

## FTD vs. Normal Market Activity

- **Normal trading:** Settle smoothly (T+2).
- **FTD Events:** Indicate breakdowns, often tied to:
    - Operational problems.
    - Financial stress.
    - Potential manipulation.
    - High volatility periods.

## Key Correlations \& Patterns

1. **Crisis-Driven FTD Spikes**
    - Financial Crisis (2008), Pandemic (2020), Corporate Crisis (2015)
2. **Quarterly Timing**
    - Q2/Q3 usual peaks, Q4 drops (year-end adjustments)
3. **Performance Disconnect**
    - High FTD amid strong corporate operations, denoting market skepticism (esp. 2020, 5G growth)
4. **Leadership \& Strategic Changes**
    - FTD spikes often precede or coincide with leadership changes (e.g., 2015-16)
5. **Short-Selling Pressure**
    - High FTD values likely reflect increased short selling.
